Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 15, 2026

Notchman turns text you choose into a short spoken summary (TL;DR) or reads it aloud. This Privacy Policy explains what personal data that involves, why we use it, who helps us, how long we keep it and the rights you have, wherever you live, including under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP Act) and Information Technology Act, 2000, the EU and UK General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), and US state privacy laws such as California's CCPA/CPRA. It applies to the Notchman apps for iPhone, Mac and Windows, the Community edition, our servers and notchman.app (the "Service"), and forms part of our Terms of Service.

The short version: Notchman only looks at what you choose, when you press TL;DR or Read, share something to it, or pick text on your screen. It never watches your clipboard, screen or keyboard in the background. Our server doesn't keep the text you send or the audio it makes. We don't sell your data, don't use it for advertising, don't use ads or cross-site trackers, and don't use your text to train AI models.

1. Who we are

Notchman is run by Ritu Patil, an individual based in Surat, Gujarat, India ("Notchman", "we", "us"). We are the controller (under the DPDP Act, the "data fiduciary") of the personal data described here. Contact: ritu@miniclayai.com.

2. What we process, why, and our legal basis

  • Text you choose to listen to. When you press TL;DR, the text (or the text of a link, which your device fetches from that website) is sent over an encrypted connection to our server, which asks our AI provider (Groq) to summarize it. To hear it in a natural voice, the text to be spoken is sent to our voice provider (ElevenLabs, or Microsoft Azure AI Speech for the standard voice). Purpose: to provide the feature you asked for. Legal basis: your consent, which you give in the app before your first TL;DR, and performance of our contract with you (GDPR Art. 6(1)(a) and (b); DPDP Act s. 6). Our server processes this text in memory and does not store it or the audio.
  • A random install ID. Each copy of the app creates a random identifier that isn't linked to your name, email or device identifiers (unless you buy a plan on Mac or Windows: then it's kept with the email you gave, see below). Our server uses it to count your daily and monthly usage, keep usage fair and apply your plan. Once a day the desktop app also sends it with the app's version and platform, so we can count installs and how many people open the app; only a one-way hash of it is kept for that. Legal basis: contract and our legitimate interest in running and improving the Service and preventing abuse.
  • A computer fingerprint for fair free limits (Mac and Windows). The desktop app sends a one-way, Notchman-specific hash of your computer's built-in ID, so reinstalling the app doesn't reset the Free plan, and so a plan you buy stays on that computer after a reinstall. The real ID never leaves your computer and the hash can't be turned back into it or matched with other apps. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in preventing abuse of the Free plan, and contract.
  • Your network address. Our server sees your IP address with each request, like any website. To stop one network from using up the Free plan with many installs, it counts free use per network per day under a one-way hash of the address; we don't store the address itself. Legal basis: legitimate interests (abuse prevention and security).
  • Usage counts. Numbers such as "3 TL;DRs today" or "voice characters this month", stored against the install ID or subscription. Legal basis: contract.
  • Your email, when you buy on Mac or Windows. Before checkout, the desktop app asks for your email. We fill it in on the payment page and keep it on our server with your install ID, the computer hash above, the plan and the time, so we can send your receipt, keep your plan working, and help you (for example if a payment doesn't go through or you need your plan moved to another computer). We may write to you about your purchase; we never send marketing emails without asking and never sell or share your email for advertising. Legal basis: contract (steps before and for your purchase).
  • Subscription status. If you subscribe, we receive proof of purchase from Apple (iPhone) or from Dodo Payments (Mac and Windows), including the email you paid with, to know which plan you're on and when it renews. We don't receive your card details. Legal basis: contract and legal obligations (tax and accounting).
  • Website visits (notchman.app). To see how people use the site, it sends anonymous events: the page, the site that linked to it (only its domain), campaign tags in the link, whether you're on a phone or computer, which buttons were clicked, which downloads were started, how far the page was scrolled, which FAQ answers were opened and how long the page was open. Our server keeps only daily totals. There are no cookies and nothing is stored on your device; unique visitors and downloads are counted with a one-way code made from the day, IP address and browser, which changes every day and can't be traced back to you. For a few key actions (tapping Get Notchman Pro, a download button or the GitHub link) we also keep the time to the minute, the site that sent the visit, its campaign tag and whether it was a phone or a computer, so we can see what leads people to try Notchman; nothing that identifies you is kept with it. If your browser sends Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control, no events are sent. The site loads its fonts from Google Fonts, which receives your IP address like any download. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in improving the site.
  • App usage statistics (iPhone). To learn which parts of the app help people and where they get stuck, so we can make it easier to use, the app sends short event names such as "opened the app", "finished onboarding step 2", "TL;DR from the Dynamic Island", "opened the plans", "tapped Subscribe", "purchase completed" or "a link couldn't be read". It never sends what you copy, read or listen to. Our server keeps daily totals, and counts how many people did each thing with a one-way code made from the day and your install ID, which changes every day and isn't kept longer than two days. For the purchase steps it also keeps the time to the minute. This is used only to improve Notchman: never for advertising, never sold, and never combined with data from other apps or websites (no tracking). You can turn it off anytime in Settings › Share Usage Statistics. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in improving the app, and your consent in the app (DPDP Act).
  • Technical logs. Our server keeps limited operational logs (time, request type, errors) to keep the Service running and secure. They don't contain the text you send. Legal basis: legitimate interests (security and reliability).
  • Support and legal requests. If you email us, we use your email address and message to reply and to keep a record of the request. Legal basis: legitimate interests and legal obligations.

We don't make decisions about you based solely on automated processing that have legal or similarly significant effects, and we don't build advertising profiles.

3. What stays on your device

  • Your listening history, saved audio, settings, and the record of when you accepted these terms.
  • On iPhone, your Sign in with Apple details (name and email, if you choose to share them). They are kept in your iPhone's keychain and not sent to our server.
  • Screenshots you use with Notchman are read on your device and never uploaded.
  • On Mac and Windows, "Pick on screen" takes a screenshot and reads its text on your computer; the screenshot is never uploaded. When you click TL;DR or Read on the Shiba, Notchman copies the text you selected (as if you pressed Ctrl+C or ⌘C) and then restores your clipboard.
  • On Mac and Windows, the free voice runs on your computer. The first time it's used, the app downloads the open-source voice model from Hugging Face and supporting files from jsDelivr, which see your IP address like any download.
  • To show app and site logos, your device looks up an app's name on Apple's App Store or fetches a website's icon. Only the app name or website address is sent, never your messages.
  • The Community edition sends your text directly from your computer to the providers whose API keys you enter (for example Groq or ElevenLabs), under their terms; it doesn't send anything to us.

4. Who helps us

We share personal data only with service providers that process it on our behalf and under contract, or as required by law:

  • Amazon Web Services: hosting our server, usage counts and app downloads (United States).
  • Groq: AI summaries.
  • ElevenLabs: the natural Notchman voice.
  • Microsoft Azure AI Speech: the standard voice, when the natural voice isn't used.
  • Apple: App Store purchases, Sign in with Apple, TestFlight.
  • Dodo Payments: our merchant of record for Mac and Windows subscriptions. It processes your payment details, email and billing address under its own privacy policy.
  • Vercel: hosting notchman.app. Google Fonts: the website's fonts.

These providers process text only to perform their service and may keep it for a limited time for abuse monitoring, security or legal reasons as described in their own policies. We may also disclose data if required by law, court order or a lawful request from authorities, to protect our rights, users or the public, or to a successor if the Service is transferred (with this policy continuing to apply).

We do not sell personal data, and we do not "share" it for cross-context behavioural advertising or use it for targeted advertising, as those terms are defined in US state laws.

5. How long we keep data

  • Text you send and the audio made from it: not stored by our server; processed only while your request is handled.
  • Daily usage counts and per-network counts: deleted automatically after about 2 days. Monthly counts: after about 40 days.
  • Install, app usage and website statistics: daily totals (and the minute of key actions) for up to about 13 months; the daily one-way codes used to count people for about 2 days; the one-way code used to count an install once for up to about 13 months.
  • Subscription records: while your subscription is active and afterwards as long as needed for tax, accounting and legal purposes.
  • The email you give before checkout, if you don't buy: deleted automatically after about 13 months, or sooner if you ask.
  • Technical logs: up to about 30 days, unless needed longer to investigate abuse or a security incident.
  • Support emails: as long as needed to handle your request and any follow-up, normally no more than 2 years.
  • Data on your device: until you delete it in the app or uninstall the app.

6. International transfers

Our providers may process data in the United States and other countries. Where the law requires it, for example for transfers out of the EU, UK or India, we rely on lawful transfer mechanisms such as the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses and our providers' contractual commitments, and we don't transfer data to countries restricted by the Government of India.

7. Your rights and choices

Depending on where you live, you can ask to access, correct, complete, update, delete or receive a copy of your personal data, object to or restrict its processing, and withdraw consent at any time (without affecting processing before withdrawal). Specifically:

  • India (DPDP Act, 2023): the right to a summary of your personal data and how it's processed, to correction, completion, updating and erasure, to grievance redressal, and to nominate someone to exercise your rights if you die or become incapacitated.
  • EU, EEA and UK (GDPR): the rights above, plus data portability and the right to complain to your local data protection authority.
  • United States: residents of California and other states with consumer privacy laws have the right to know, access, correct and delete personal data, to opt out of sale, sharing, targeted advertising and profiling (we do none of these), and not to be discriminated against for using these rights. You can appeal our decision by replying to it. We treat Global Privacy Control signals as an opt-out request.

You can stop app usage statistics anytime in the iPhone app's Settings › Share Usage Statistics, and the website sends nothing if your browser uses Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control.

Unless you bought or started buying a plan (then we keep the email you gave), we don't keep your name or email on our server, and most of your data is on your own device; you can delete it by clearing your history in the app or uninstalling it. For anything else, email ritu@miniclayai.com and include your install ID (shown in the app's Settings). We answer within one month (45 days where US state law allows) and may need to verify your request.

8. Grievance Officer (India)

Ritu Patil, Grievance Officer, ritu@miniclayai.com, Surat, Gujarat, India. We acknowledge complaints within 48 hours and aim to resolve them within 15 days. If you're not satisfied with our response, you may complain to the Data Protection Board of India.

9. Children

Notchman is not directed to children under 13, and in the EU/EEA it's for people old enough to consent to online services in their country (up to 16). In India, it is not intended for anyone under 18 without verifiable consent from a parent or guardian, and we don't track or monitor children's behaviour or target advertising at them. We don't knowingly collect children's data; if you believe a child has used Notchman without the required consent, contact us and we'll delete what we hold.

10. Security and breaches

Data is encrypted in transit, API keys stay on our server, access to our systems is limited and protected, and the apps store data in your device's protected storage. No method of transmission or storage is completely secure, but we take reasonable security safeguards to protect your data. If a personal data breach occurs, we'll notify you and the relevant authorities (including the Data Protection Board of India and CERT-In) where the law requires.

11. Sensitive content and other people's data

Don't use Notchman on passwords, one-time codes, payment card numbers, health, financial or other sensitive information you don't want an AI service to process. If text you send contains other people's personal data (for example a group chat), you are responsible for being allowed to share it. Either way, it's processed only to give you the summary or voice you asked for.

12. Your consent and how to withdraw it

Before you use the Service, the app asks you to agree to the Terms of Service and this Privacy Policy, and before your first TL;DR it explains that your text is sent to our AI and voice providers. A record of when and to which version you agreed is kept on your device. You can withdraw your consent at any time by stopping use of the cloud features, deleting your history and uninstalling the app; on iPhone you can also switch to the on-device voice where available. Withdrawing consent doesn't affect processing that happened before.

13. Changes to this policy

We'll update this policy when our practices or the law change, and show the new date at the top. For significant changes we'll tell you in the app or on this website, and ask for your consent again where the law requires.
